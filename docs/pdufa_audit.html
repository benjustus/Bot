Pre-PDUFA Run-up: real pattern, no real edge

A red-team replication and 27×-larger extension of the original 16-event backtest. The strategy: buy 30 days before a drug's FDA decision date, sell 7 days before. Does it actually beat the biotech index? Short answer below.

Verdict

The raw run-up is genuine but small (+3.4%/trade) — and it is almost entirely biotech-sector beta. Adjusted for XBI, the PDUFA-specific excess is +1.0% and statistically indistinguishable from zero.

On 431 verified events, the excess hit-rate is a coin flip (49.7%), the median excess is ~0%, and eventual rejections (CRLs) ran up as much as approvals. In the same window, simply holding XBI returned +76.5% — beating the strategy at half the risk.

So where's the problem?

  • n = 16. The sign-test on the original returns gives p = 0.21 — not significant even before adjustment.
  • Winner selection. Only known, mostly-approved drugs; 7 acquired winners dropped; zero CRLs.
  • No beta control. 2023–25 was a strong biotech tape — any name held 23 days made money.
  • One regime. The sample sits inside a bull market (see §5).

In the <€500M tier the mean is +7.6% but the median is +1.5% — a handful of extreme, often data-suspect names (e.g. RNAZ, a preclinical company with no real PDUFA and ±100% reverse-split prints) do all the work. In big caps the excess is exactly zero, as expected: one drug doesn't move a mega-cap. This is lottery-ticket variance, not a durable edge — and §7 shows real trading costs erase it precisely here.

05

It's a regime, not a strategy

pdufa.bio's own 1,754-event study (T-120→T-1) and my 2022–23 approvals cohort agree: the run-up flips negative in bear years.

Pre-decision run-up by year (external reference, T-120→T-1). Strongly positive in 2020/2024/2025 bull tapes; negative in the 2022–23 bear. My own approvals-only 2022–23 cohort (winner-biased, the optimistic case) came out flat: raw +2.4%, median +0.07%, hit 51.5%, not significant.

06

The decisive comparison: just buy the index

Over the exact span of the capital simulation (Dec 2024 → Jun 2026), what did passive beats active?

Buy-and-hold return over the same window.

XBI +76.5% (−26% drawdown) beat the all-in strategy's Monte-Carlo median of +49% — with half the drawdown and no single-name gap risk.

The strategy's positive median is sector beta you could own directly. Across 3,000 random roll-over chains: median €2,242, but the 5th percentile is €1,143 (−24%), the worst path €598 (−60%), and 18% of paths lose money. You'd take concentrated single-stock risk to underperform the index.

08

If you insisted on trading it

There is no scientifically defensible long-only run-up strategy in this evidence. The raw signal is biotech beta — cheaper and safer via XBI. The only rigorous framing left would be: test for residual alpha beta-neutral (long name / short XBI — my numbers say ≈none); use fixed small size (≤1–2%), never all-in (the −60% ruin tail); hard exit before the event; exclude nano-caps and reverse-split names; and validate across a full bull-and-bear cycle, not one regime. Prediction: net of costs, still no durable edge.

Honest limits. The clean dual-outcome sample is concentrated in 2025–26 (a bull regime); a fully unbiased 2010–24 set was not reconstructable from free sources (ex-ante PDUFA calendars are paywalled; the Web Archive was blocked here).
